Word Help

Gross domestic product (GDP)
The dollar value of all final goods and services produced within a country's borders in a given year.
Final goods
Products in the form sold to consumers, like a finished computer.
Intermediate goods
Products used to make final goods, like the memory chips inside a computer. They are not counted, so nothing gets counted twice.
Durable goods
Goods that last a long time, like a refrigerator.
Nondurable goods
Goods that last a short time, like food or sneakers.
The four spending categories
Consumer goods and services, business goods and services, government goods and services, and net exports. Add all four to get GDP.
Net exports
Exports (made here, bought in other countries) minus imports (made in other countries, bought here).
Nominal GDP
GDP measured in current prices: this year's quantities times this year's prices.
Real GDP
GDP in constant prices: this year's quantities times the base year's prices. It shows if output really grew.
Base year
The year whose prices are used to measure real GDP. On your island, it is Year 1.
Price level
The overall level of prices in the economy.
Gross national product (GNP)
The value of goods and services produced by a nation's citizens and firms, even if made in another country.
Nonmarket activities
Work people do for themselves for free, like mowing their own lawn. Not counted in GDP.
Underground economy
Sales that are never reported to the government, like cash "under the table." Not counted in GDP.
Negative externalities
Harmful side effects, like pollution, that are not subtracted from GDP.
