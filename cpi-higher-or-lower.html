Which prices are climbing fastest?

The Consumer Price Index (CPI) tracks the cost of a "market basket" of goods and services that typical urban households buy. When the CPI rises, the overall price level is rising: that is inflation.

Every number in this game is real government data: the 12-month percent change in prices from September 2025 to September 2026. A positive number means prices went up. A negative number means prices went down.

Round 1: Beat the Headline. Overall Midwest prices rose 4.2%. Did each item rise faster or slower than that?
Round 2: Head to Head. Classic higher or lower. Did the next item's price rise more or less than the last one?
Round 3: Midwest vs. Nation. Did prices here in the Midwest (including Indiana) rise faster or slower than the U.S. average?

What the data shows

  • One number hides a lot. Overall Midwest inflation was 4.2%, but individual items ranged from about -2% to +36%.
  • Energy did the heavy lifting. Gasoline rose about 35%. Remove food and energy and Midwest inflation drops to 3.0% (U.S.: 2.6%). That smaller number is called core inflation.
  • Falling prices are not deflation. Used cars, furniture, and car insurance got cheaper, but deflation only happens when the overall price level falls.
  • Where you live matters. The Midwest (4.2%) ran hotter than the nation (3.7%), largely because of electricity and housing costs.
Discuss: Think about what YOU spend money on. Would your personal inflation rate be higher or lower than 4.2%? Which single category would affect you the most?
Data: U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, CPI-U, 12-month percent change to September 2026, not seasonally adjusted.
