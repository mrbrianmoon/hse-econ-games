Word Help

Business cycle
A period of expansion followed by a period of contraction in the economy.
Expansion
A period of economic growth, measured by rising real GDP. Jobs are plentiful.
Peak
The height of an expansion, when real GDP stops rising.
Contraction
An economic decline marked by falling real GDP. Unemployment usually rises.
Trough
The lowest point of a contraction. Real GDP stops falling and a new expansion begins.
Recession
When real GDP falls for two quarters in a row (at least six months).
Depression
An especially long and severe recession, with high unemployment and low output.
Stagflation
Falling real GDP combined with a rising price level (inflation).
Business investment
Businesses spending on new plants and equipment. Rising investment points to expansion.
Interest rates
The cost of borrowing. Rising rates mean people and businesses borrow and spend less, which points to contraction.
Consumer expectations
How people feel about the future. Rising confidence means more spending, which points to expansion.
External shocks
Big surprise events like oil supply cuts, wars, droughts, or a huge discovery.
Leading indicators
Data, like the stock market, that usually changes BEFORE the economy does. Used to forecast.
